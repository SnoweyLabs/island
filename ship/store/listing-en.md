## Description (up to 10,000 characters)

Seeing and switching your browser tabs needs the free Island add-on for Chrome, which is a separate download in the Chrome Web Store. Everything else in Island works without it.

Island is a small glass island at the top of your screen. One key brings it, and it keeps the things you use all day in one place: the programs, the folders and the websites you choose, on pages with colours of your own.

What it does:
- Shows what is open. A thing that is closed is grey; a program with several windows shows one dot for each window.
- A click starts a program, opens a folder or a site, or brings the window you already have to the front. Each page and each thing can have a key of its own.
- A close button closes the window you just brought forward (never a program that runs with administrator rights).
- Search in the island itself: type, and the matches appear as round tiles; Enter opens the first one.
- Scenes: one key opens several things together and brings forward the ones that are already open.
- A small pill appears while something plays, with the name, previous, play or pause, next and search, and an edge that shows how much is left.
- Three modes. Focus lets the notice that your coding agent has finished appear even over a fullscreen program. Vibe keeps everything that appears by itself away from fullscreen programs. Do not disturb shows nothing by itself.
- Tells you when Claude Code has finished or needs your answer, once you connect it from the settings screen. Island shows exactly what it will add to Claude Code's settings and asks first.
- Stays away from games and other fullscreen programs, and has a list of programs it never appears over.
- Starts with Windows, if you switch that on. A short setup of five steps the first time, and a settings screen you can reach at any time.

Your things stay on your computer. Island does not connect to the internet and has no account. It keeps a few small files of your choices on your computer, and nothing you type into its search is written anywhere. The privacy policy says exactly what is kept.

Made and tried on one computer so far: it has not yet been tried on other computers.
